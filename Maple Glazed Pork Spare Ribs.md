# Maple Syrup Ribs (Kamado, Low and Slow)

## Meat
- 2 racks British pork spare ribs (meatier than US St Louis cut — allow extra time)
- Remove the membrane from the bone side

## Dry Rub
- 2 tbsp soft brown sugar
- 1 tbsp smoked paprika
- 1 tbsp fine sea salt
- 2 tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp garlic granules
- 1 tsp onion granules
- ½ tsp mustard powder

## Maple Glaze
- 100ml maple syrup (use the real stuff, not maple-flavoured syrup)
- 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- Pinch of chilli flakes (optional)

## Method

### Prep
1. Remove membrane, pat ribs dry, apply rub generously on both sides.
2. Rest at room temperature for 30–45 minutes while the kamado comes up.

### Kamado Setup
- Heat deflector in, indirect
- Target 110–120°C, stable before the meat goes on
- 2–3 chunks of smoking wood — apple or cherry pair well with maple

### Cook
1. Ribs on bone-side down. Leave alone for 3 hours.
2. Wrap at the stall (~70°C internal) in foil with a splash of apple juice
   and a drizzle of the maple syrup. Return to the kamado.
3. Cook wrapped until internal hits 86–88°C (roughly 1.5–2 hours).
4. Unwrap, brush generously with the glaze, and return unwrapped for
   30–45 minutes until the glaze sets and internal reaches 88–92°C.
5. Glaze again in the final 10 minutes if you want it stickier.

### Doneness
- Pull at 88–92°C internal
- Bend test: rack should crack on the surface when lifted from one end
- Rest 15 minutes, loosely tented, before cutting

## Timing
- Total: 5.5–6.5 hours for British spare ribs (add up to an hour vs
  American cuts due to the extra meat)

## Notes
- Simmer the glaze ingredients for 2–3 minutes to thicken slightly before brushing
- Don't glaze earlier than the final 45 minutes — maple syrup burns above 120°C surface heat, so keep the kamado at the low end once it's on
- Baby back ribs work too but knock 1–1.5 hours off and wrap earlier
- Total cook time will vary — go by temperature and the bend test, not the clock.
- If your kamado runs hotter than 120°C, that's fine up to 130°C; just check earlier.
